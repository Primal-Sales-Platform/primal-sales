[ THE PART WORTH THINKING ABOUT ]

Your CRM is keeping three records. Your reports read one of them.

The fair objection is that if nobody keeps the CRM current, nobody can get a real number out of it either. That's the right question, and it's the whole thing.

The first record is what your team typed. The stage, the close reason picked off a dropdown, whatever they were told to keep current. That one takes discipline, it's half empty, and it's the only one your reports are built on.

The second is what the system logged by itself. When the deal was created. When it moved. When it stopped moving. Nobody types a timestamp and nobody can massage one.

The third is what your buyers did. Whether anyone wrote back. Whether they showed up. Whether they booked the time themselves. Along with everything said on the calls and sitting in the email threads, which your CRM stores and never reads.

Any one of them on its own will lie to you.

A deal sitting untouched for 200 days isn't dead if the prospect wrote back last week. A deal that moved through three stages isn't alive if no human ever answered. You need all three, read against each other, and that is the entire job.

It's how 272 of my 336 losses turned out to be deals that never happened. One record said I lost them. The other two said nobody ever spoke to them.
