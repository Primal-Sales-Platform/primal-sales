Illustrative sample review · fictional call and fictional output · not a customer result

Discovery call · outcome unknown

A buyer agrees with the plan. Capacity is the sticking point.

The rep connects the agency’s service to the buyer’s workload. When the buyer raises an onboarding concern, the rep offers to revisit later without checking what work would actually block a start.

What worked

“If we owned the weekly creative testing, what would that give your team back?”

The rep asks the buyer to describe the value in operational terms. The buyer answers: “Time to get the launch out the door.” That gives the rep a concrete benefit to return to.

Where the response could be stronger

“The plan makes sense. I’m worried about taking on another onboarding project during the launch.”

The rep replies: “We can reconnect after launch.” The conversation moves from evaluating a start to delaying it before the onboarding concern is explored.

See a stronger response to practice
“It sounds like the onboarding work is the concern. You said getting creative testing off your plate would free up time for the launch. Which parts of getting started would put more work on your team? Let’s map those out and see whether there’s a manageable way to start.”

This acknowledges the concern, returns to the buyer’s stated benefit, and tests the blocker. It does not assume an onboarding time commitment the rep has not established.

What to practice next

Rehearse the timing objection. Reflect the concern, connect back to the buyer’s own reason for change, and ask what specifically prevents starting. Agree on a next step after understanding the answer.

Context to watch

The buyer has not rejected the plan. The effort required to onboard is unresolved. The excerpt does not establish a signed deal, a lost deal, or a proven pattern across the team.

Before you send a call

A few straight answers.

What happens to my recording?

Uploaded recordings are automatically removed from upload storage after transcription. Failed or abandoned uploads are retried by scheduled cleanup. Submitted transcripts and reviews remain until expiry or deletion. Unconverted previews expire after 30 days; use the deletion control on your results page or the link in your email to delete sooner.

How long does it take?

Pasting a transcript skips transcription. Audio and video are transcribed first; processing time varies with call length and the file. Your email return link lets you come back to the review.

What can I upload or paste?

Start with a complete sales-call transcript, upload audio or video, or select a transcript from a supported recorder. The submission page shows the accepted file formats and limits.

Do I need Fathom or Fireflies?

No. You can paste a transcript or upload a recording. Connecting a supported recorder is another way to select the calls you want reviewed.

Will I have to book a demo or sales call?

No. Enter your email to open the review and get a return link. You also receive a short follow-up series from Ace, your personalized AI coach, with an unsubscribe option.

What if I only have one or two reps?

You can still use the free review. This page is focused on agency owners and managers with three or more closers, but a smaller team can start with one complete call.

Why is the sample fictional?

It lets you inspect the format without presenting an invented customer result as proof. The dialogue and output are labeled fictional throughout.

Will one call produce my team’s script and methodology?

The free review provides evidence and coaching from the calls you submit. Building a shared methodology, non-negotiables, course, and script requires a broader call library and your team’s process.
